## Surprised us

- **A phone bug that was not in the component everyone would have suspected.**
  Choosing a sort on the Week Board left the whole page zoomed out. The listbox
  was innocent. `.visually-hidden` sets `width:1px`; every select it hides also
  wears `.week-select`, which sets `min-width:160px`; **min-width beats width**.
  `clip` and `clip-path` stop the box being painted and do not remove it from
  the page's scrollable overflow, and with no `left`/`top` it sits at its static
  position. At 430px the Week Board's filter row does not wrap, so the phantom
  select ran to x=533 against a 430 viewport: `scrollWidth` 533, and the fixed
  `.bottom-nav` stretched to match. Width-dependent, which is why it read as
  intermittent and never showed on
  a desktop width — at 375px the row wraps and the select lands inside the
  viewport. Four selects carried it; one was positioned to trigger it.
- **A before/after table is the highest-risk artifact here, and #73's had a row
  measured at the wrong viewport.** The table was headed "430x900" and claimed
  `.game-card` went 351 to 398. It did not: 351 is the card at 375px and 398 is
  the card at 430px, on the same unfixed build. The card is the viewport minus
  the page gutters and never depended on the fix. Booth could not reproduce it,
  gave the right mechanism (`.game-grid` is one column at either width), and was
  correct. The two columns of a table are measured minutes apart under
  conditions the header states only once, so a row that moved for an unrelated
  reason is indistinguishable from one that moved for the reason the table is
  about. Three of Booth's four discrepancies on #73 were pixel-level
  environment differences; this one was mine.
- **Three mis-scoped figures in PR bodies in one day, and writing the trap entry
  did not stop the next one.** #71 said "five of the eight new tests" were red;
  it was six of eight, because the red run happened when only seven guards
  existed and the eighth was added afterwards in response to a mutation — the
  numerator from one moment, the denominator from another. #72 said "42
  mutations over the two affected files" from `--id "p*"`: the glob pulled 38
  cases from five untouched files and missed 7 of the 11 that mattered, so it
  was simultaneously too wide and too narrow. Both were caught by Booth, both
  were in the body and neither in the code, and the second shipped after the
  first was already written into CLAUDE.md. A trap entry is not a control; the
  mechanical fix belongs in `scout_preflight.py`.
- **A mutation written before its guard walked through every other test in the
  file.** Dropping `teamDiveListbox = ` while keeping `enhanceSelect(select)`
  leaves the listbox built and correct on load, leaves `enhanceSelect(` for the
  coverage guard and the literal `refresh()` for the branch guard, and removes
  the only thing that could call it. The guard that catches it ties the handle
  and the refresh together by name, and exists only because the mutation was
  imagined first.

## Second session — the first graded week, and the page that did not rebuild

The weekly run fired on schedule at 11:05 UTC and did its job: Week 1 graded
(Model A 11/16, Model B 11/16, market 12/16), Week 2 locked in, SOS populated,
the "no games played yet" note retired itself. The reported symptom was the
opposite — "grading did not happen, and there is no Week 2" — because the
published page was still the 04:30 UTC build.

**The defect: `deploy-pages.yml`'s `workflow_run` list named only the
collector.** The weekly workflow commits with the default `GITHUB_TOKEN`,
which cannot trigger another workflow, so its `paths:` match counts for
nothing. This is the 2026-09-09 severed handoff, in a second workflow. Fixed
in #74.

What makes it worth recording is not the defect but its guard.
`tests/test_generated_data_reaches_the_page.py` was written FOR this exact
failure mode and did not catch it, because `DASHBOARD_INPUTS` named one file
and `COLLECTOR` named one workflow. Its own docstring said "the check is on
the chain, not on either end of it" — and there were two chains. A guard that
names its subject instead of enumerating the class is a guard scoped to the
instance that prompted it. The rewrite discovers the writers and reads the
watched path set out of the builder, so the builder stays the single source of
truth.

**The vacuity test paid for itself inside ten minutes.** The first draft of
the writer scan returned nothing — `- 'src/**'` had its quotes stripped before
its leading dash, so every root came out as `'src` and no intersection ever
matched. The bridging assertion PASSED over that empty list. Had I written
only the assertion I meant to write, the guard would have shipped green,
proving nothing, for the second time in the same file's history. Whenever an
assertion is over a list some scan produces, the scan needs its own test, and
`rebuild-bridge-writer-scan-goes-blind` is now that case in the corpus.
