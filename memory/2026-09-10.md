# 2026-09-10 — Stage 8 design phase, closed

## Shipped

- No PRs. No code changed; `src/` and `tests/` untouched, suite still 704/1.
- `docs/design/STAGE8-DESIGN.md` — the visual direction as decided over four
  Fable rounds (v3.0 to v3.4), including the full token block.

## Decided

**Colour has exactly four jobs, and job 3 is load-bearing.** Structure, the
model's lean, a graded outcome, chart series identity. The graded colours may
appear **only after a game is scored** — which is why the pick emphasis added
this session uses weight and words rather than green. A colour meaning
"correct" on a game that has not happened would make the page lie.

**Cutting beat adding, twice.** Mark called the four Week Board stat tiles and
the per-card `A − MARKET` field redundant. Removing `A − MARKET` and restating
the market probability for the same team the models name made the comparison
*more* available: the gap became two numbers side by side instead of a signed
derived figure in different units. Deleting the calculation improved the page.

**The market probability names the Model A favourite — the bold row.** Not the
away team, not the underdog. Otherwise the reader subtracts from 100 in their
head on exactly the cards where the models and the market disagree.

**Home is a word, not a dangling `@`.** The old `@` was appended to the *away*
row to mean "this team is away", but the convention puts `@` before the home
team with an object (`@ MIA`), so a trailing `@` marks the team it does not
describe. At the 340px card floor a bordered tag does not fit, and on a final
game the pre-game record is dropped because the score supersedes it.

**Two encodings were built and rejected, each for a stated reason.** A `PICK`
badge: a sixth signal for a fact already encoded five ways, in the visual
language of a tout sheet, and on an `A ≠ B` card it stamps "PICK" on a row
Model B disagrees with. A perpetually spinning logo: motion in peripheral
vision on every page reads as amateur and undoes the restraint the rest of the
design earns. Building both and showing them beside the alternatives settled
each in one exchange, where prose would have gone back and forth.

**Keep wholesale `innerHTML` re-render.** At this scale it costs milliseconds
and buys total predictability — every view is a pure function of `state`, with
no reconciliation code to get wrong. The price is two rules: interaction state
lives in `state` and is re-derived, and floating UI lives outside the rendered
tree.

## Surprised us

**Re-deriving every number caught three false claims, and two of them were
mine.** Contrast recomputed from the hex values; colour-blind separation via
the Machado 2009 matrices and CIEDE2000; geometry by rendering in headless
Chromium and reading `getBoundingClientRect`. Fable claimed 4.5:1 where the
value was 4.22:1, claimed a row fit on one line where it measured 65px against
its neighbours' 48px, and claimed a ≥15 colour-blindness floor where one pair
sat at 14.7.

Then, writing the brand mark myself, I asserted in a CSS comment that three
rules shared a specificity so source order would decide. They did not:
`.brand:hover .brand-mark .spin` is four classes and my `.is-loading` rule was
three, so hovering the logo during a load froze the spinner — and because the
tumble then never ran, the "wait for the revolution to finish" logic hung and
the loading state stuck on permanently. One wrong assumption, two failures.
Later, adding the `Home` tag, I assumed home rows had spare width because the
146px measurement came from an away row; graded home rows carry a score and
overflowed at 174px. **Reading your own CSS back is not verification.**

**A specification can be wrong in a way the implementer cannot see.** I asked
for the market probability to name "the team the card's top row names". Fable
implemented that exactly — and the top row is always the away team, while the
team the eye lands on is the bold favourite. My wording, their faithful work.

**Two traps that only rendering finds.** An animated disclosure built on
`grid-template-rows: 0fr` leaves collapsed content in the accessibility tree
and in find-in-page, contradicting the button's `aria-expanded="false"`;
`display: none` had been hiding it correctly and the animation quietly removed
that. And wholesale re-render destroys interaction state — an open breakdown
vanished on a filter change, in the same deliverable whose own architecture
note recommended keeping such state in `state`.

**The repo's doc guards earned their keep.** Closing the stage, I wrote
`docs/context.md` pointing at two mock files I had not saved, and added a
memory file without indexing it. `tests/test_workflow_docs.py` failed on both
within a minute.
