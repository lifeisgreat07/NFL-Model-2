# UX/UI review, 2026-09-26

An independent review of the dashboard, written in answer to Mark's audit prompt of
2026-09-26 (a 24-section brief built around an earlier outside review that scored the
dashboard 86/100). It is a recommendation, not a decision record: nothing below is
decided until Mark agrees it, and the agreed parts become stages in CLAUDE.md.

**How it was inspected.** Not from the prompt. `index.html` was regenerated on `markys`
at `265284d` and served over localhost in the cloud sandbox, with the Plus Jakarta Sans
webfont files and the 32 ESPN team logos staged in, so every render below is in the real
typeface with real logos (CLAUDE.md's webfont trap: a render without the font can find
nothing). All nine pages were captured in headless Chromium at 1440, 1280, 1024, 768,
390 and 360 px wide, dark and light, with mobile emulation below 1024. The template,
the generator and `docs/design/STAGE8-DESIGN.md` were read. Each measured figure is listed
with how it was measured in the appendix at the end.

---

## 1. Executive assessment

The dashboard is better than most portfolio analytics projects in the one way that is
hardest to fake: it tells the truth about itself. Model Lab lists 26 rejections, Season
Accuracy leads with "the betting market leads by 1 game", and every Week Board card says
in one sentence why the model leans the way it does. The visual system is restrained
and consistent: one accent colour, a neutral ramp, tabular figures, logos as team
identity. There is no horizontal overflow on any page at any of the six widths.

What keeps it in the high 70s rather than the 90s is not polish. It is five things:

1. **The page does not open on what matters.** The default page is Power Ratings. On
   the Week Board, a first-time visitor on a phone sees no game at all on the first
   screen: the orientation banner is 610 px tall and the first card starts 70 px below
   the bottom of an 844 px screen. At 1440×900 no complete card is visible either.
2. **Two places where the UI is less honest than the engine behind it.** My Picks accepts
   a pick for a game that has already been played and graded, and counts it: picking
   every graded winner puts "My picks 100.0%, 32 of 32" on Season Accuracy. And Model
   Lab, the project's research record, contains neither Stage 5 nor this week's Stage 6
   (Next Gen Stats, `experiments/stage6`; the page's older "research Stage 6" is a
   different, retired numbering). The ten
   pre-registered Stage 5 questions and the Next Gen Stats screen live only in
   `experiments/`.
3. **One number, three meanings.** "Confidence #6 · 11 pts" on a card is a pick'em rank
   and points, not statistical confidence. "+0.83pt" in Model Lab is percentage points.
   The net rating `+0.149` has no unit anywhere on the page.
4. **The single most important input is invisible.** The quarterback matchup carries
   most of the model's weight, and the card never names either quarterback. On
   KC @ MIA this week, Model A picks MIA "almost entirely on the quarterback matchup"
   while the line has KC by 11.5, and a reader has no way to see which quarterbacks
   that sentence is about.
5. **Model Lab is a 46-row hand-written table with 11 different verdict labels,**
   led by accuracy figures that the project's own methodology says cannot carry a
   result.

None of these needs a framework, a rewrite, or a new visual language. The right plan is
integrity and hierarchy first, then two page redesigns (Week Board card, Model Lab),
then the long tail. The prompt's direction ("what matters now, explore deeper if you
want") is right. About a third of its specific features should not be built.

## 4. Where I agree with the previous review

- Model transparency, Model Lab's honest negatives, the leakage record and the
  verification page are the product's strongest assets and must stay visible.
- Colour discipline and component consistency are real. The Stage 8 rule (colour has
  four jobs) is visible on every page.
- The Week Board should be the home screen, and Team Deep-Dive is the weakest page.
- Model Lab deserves a better interaction design.
- The monolithic template is a real maintainability cost.

## 5. Where I disagree

- **86 is too high**, for the reasons in section 2. Trust/credibility at 9.8 cannot
  coexist with a picks record that counts picks made after the result.
- **Accessibility at 9.2 missed a real defect.** At 1440 px the mobile "More" sheet is
  `display:block`, moved off-screen with a transform, and neither `inert` nor
  `aria-hidden`. Tabbing from the top of the default page, the 19th Tab press puts focus
  on its "Team Deep-Dive" button, 917 px down a 900 px screen: invisible focus, five
  times over, and five duplicate links for a screen reader.
- **"Too much information competing for attention" is only true of three pages.** The
  Week Board, Model Lab and Methodology are dense; Power Ratings, Season Accuracy and My
  Picks are not. A global "progressive disclosure" pass would damage the pages that are
  already right.
- **Several proposals already exist** and the review scored as if they did not: a
  consistent Model A / Model B / Market shape system (● ■ ▲, with ◆ for My Picks) is on
  Season Accuracy; a "Biggest Model Disagreement" sort and a "Model Disagreement" filter
  are on the Week Board; calibration is on Model Lab and Season Accuracy; rank movement
  arrows are on Power Ratings; empty states were systematised in #92.
- **"Intelligence strip" above the Week Board is a decision already taken twice.** Four
  stat tiles above the filter row were cut on 2026-09-10 and four headline numbers above
  the Week Board were declined on 2026-09-23, both by Mark (STAGE8-DESIGN.md and
  CLAUDE.md's Stage 10 section record them). On 2026-09-10 three of the tiles duplicated
  the filter chips and the fourth aggregated a field that had been removed; on
  2026-09-23 the Season Accuracy scoreboard was chosen instead. The proposal adds nothing
  those decisions did not consider, and "biggest disagreement" is already a sort option
  and a filter chip.

## 6. What the previous review missed

In order of importance:

1. My Picks has no kickoff lock, so the Season Accuracy race can be won after the fact.
2. Model Lab omits Stage 5 (ten pre-registered questions) and Stage 6. The most rigorous
   work in the repository is not on the page that exists to show research.
3. Off-screen focusable navigation at desktop widths.
4. "pts" means pick'em points on the cards and percentage points in Model Lab, and the
   card's "Confidence" is a rank, not a statistical confidence.
5. Net rating has no unit. `+0.149` is EPA per play, a unit the reader-facing pages are
   not allowed to name (the plain-language guard bans "EPA" there), so it is shown bare.
6. The card never names the quarterbacks.
7. A game that has been played but not yet graded (the Thursday game on a Saturday) looks
   exactly like an upcoming one. Grading runs on Tuesday.
8. The card's "Illustrative margin" presents a margin the project tested and rejected as
   a model; and its `~` renders like a minus at card size ("KC by ~10.1").
9. The sidebar footer leaks build internals: "Trained on: current nflfastR history",
   "3 week(s) saved", "Latest: 2026_week3".
10. Team Deep-Dive opens on Arizona because it is first alphabetically.
11. Model Lab's 11 verdict labels against the five the project defines.

## 8. Proposed target product vision

The philosophy in the prompt is right, with one amendment. "Here is what matters right
now. Explore deeper if you want to understand why" should read: **here is what matters
right now, here is how sure we are, and the proof is one tap away.** The second clause
is what separates this from a tipster site; the third is what separates it from a
dashboard that hides its limitations to look cleaner.

The four-level hierarchy is the right structure, and the product is already 80% built
on it. The mapping:

| Level | Question | Where it lives today | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What should I know? | Card headline and bars | Add the market as a probability; one spectrum instead of two bars |
| 2 | Why? | The "why" sentence on the card | Name the quarterbacks |
| 3 | The numbers | "+ the numbers behind this" | A mirrored contribution row per team |
| 4 | The science | Model Lab, Methodology, verification | Generate Model Lab from the experiment records |

**Visual language:** keep Stage 8's. Dark-primary, one accent, neutral ramp, elevation
by border, tabular figures, logos for teams. Linear/Stripe restraint is already the
register. What changes is emphasis, not style: one dominant number per game, fewer
nested boxes on the long pages.

**Information density:** high on Power Ratings and Model Lab (experts scan tables),
medium on the Week Board (one decision per card), low on the landing moment.

**Charts:** vanilla SVG, as now. Every chart answers a question stated in its title;
no chart without a comparison or a reference line.

## 9. Desktop architecture

- **Landing:** the Week Board for the latest week.
- **Sidebar** as now, grouped by question (This Week, Your Picks, Track Record, How It
  Was Built), with the Week Board first and a plain model-provenance line in the footer
  in place of the build internals ("Model v2.5 · trained on 2020–2025 · updated Sat
  18:19 UTC").
- **Routing:** hash routes so every page, week, team and experiment has a URL (section 12).
- **Disclosure:** inline expansion on the card, no drawers. A drawer is a second
  navigation model to maintain for content that fits inline.
- **Breakpoints** as built in Stage 10 (sidebar hands over at 1079 px; full team names
  drop at 1180 px), which already cover the six widths without overflow.

## 10. Mobile architecture

- **Bottom navigation** exists (Ratings, Board, Picks, Accuracy, More). Reorder to
  **Board, Ratings, Picks, Accuracy, More**, so the thumb's first target is the home page.
- **The first screen shows a game.** The orientation becomes three lines with a "How to
  read this" link, dismissed per device, and it sits below the first row of games on a
  phone, not above.
- **Cards stay cards** on a phone. Tables keep their own horizontal scroll with the
  scroll-edge shadow from #91; no table becomes cards except Model Lab's (section 11).
- **Every hover has a tap equivalent.** Toggle-tips, never hover-only tooltips.
- **Touch targets ≥ 24 px** everywhere (WCAG 2.5.8), and larger for primary actions
  such as the pick buttons.
- **Sticky time-slot filters (1 PM / 4 PM / SNF): rejected.** With 14 to 16 games a week
  they filter to three or four cards, which the chronological order already groups.

## 11. Page-by-page redesign recommendations

### Week Board

- **Current problem.** No game above the fold; two equal-weight bars per card make Model
  A and Model B look like peers of equal standing; the market appears only as a spread;
  quarterbacks unnamed; "Confidence" means rank; played-but-ungraded looks upcoming;
  cards are 481 px tall at 1440 px.
- **Target experience.** Scan the week in one screen of cards on desktop, one card per
  screen on a phone; each card answers "who, how likely, do the sources agree, why".
- **Desktop design.** Keep the three-column grid (it fits 14 to 16 games with no
  pagination). Per card:
  - Header: `KC at MIA`, kickoff, and a status word: *upcoming*, *played, graded Tuesday*,
    or *final* with the score once graded.
  - **One probability spectrum** from away team to home team with three markers:
    Model A ●, Model B ■, Market ▲. The headline number above it is Model B's (the
    stronger predictor on the project's own evidence), stated for the team it favours.
  - Quarterbacks named under the teams (the announced starters the pick used).
  - The why sentence, unchanged.
  - Facts row: `Line KC −11.5 · Pick'em rank 1 (16 points)`.
  - **Decision for Mark:** the spectrum replaces the team-colour split bars that were
    kept on 2026-09-21. Team colour can survive as the two end caps of the spectrum, or
    the bars can stay and the spectrum can sit under them; both should be rendered side
    by side before choosing, as that decision was.
- **Mobile design.** Same card, full width; spectrum full width; the numbers stay behind
  the toggle.
- **New components.** `probSpectrum(game)`, a status chip, a QB line.
- **Remove or simplify.** The second bar; "Illustrative margin"; the six-bullet banner.
- **Interactions.** Existing sort and filter controls stay. `[` and `]` step weeks on
  desktop (the only keyboard shortcut worth adding).
- **Accessibility.** The spectrum carries an accessible name stating all three
  probabilities in words; markers are distinguishable by shape alone.

### Power Ratings

- **Current problem.** Numbers with no unit; `−0.17 0 +0.17` axis labels on the bar
  header; no sense of this week's movement beyond ▲▼.
- **Target.** A league table a fan can read: rank, team, a rating in understandable units,
  movement.
- **Desktop.** Show the rating as **points per 100 plays** (EPA per play × 100: `+14.9`
  instead of `+0.149`), a display-only rescaling, stated once in the header's
  one-line description and held by a test so the page and the data cannot drift. Add a
  numeric week-over-week rank change (`▲2`) beside the existing arrow. A "Biggest movers"
  line above the table (three teams up, three down) answers "what changed" without a
  new page.
- **Mobile.** Rank, team, rating and change visible; offence, defence, SOS and playoff
  odds scroll as now.
- **Sparklines: defer** until at least four 2026 weeks exist. Two points are a line
  segment that looks like a trend.
- **Remove.** Nothing.
- **Reject.** Conference and division filters, comparison mode.
- **Accessibility.** Units spelled out in the column header's accessible name.

### Team Deep-Dive

- **Current problem.** Net rating only, on half the width; arbitrary default team; the
  games table has no column headings; reached from nowhere by URL.
- **Target.** "How good is this team, is it getting better, and why."
- **Desktop.** Header with rank, rating and change; a trajectory chart (2020–2025
  season ends, then 2026 weekly) with offence and defence as two lines and net as the
  bar; the games table with headings (week, opponent, line, model probability, result).
  Needs the generator to carry offence and defence per snapshot (it carries net only
  today; this is a data-file change, not a model change).
- **Mobile.** The same, stacked; the chart full width with a data table behind a toggle.
- **Default team:** the one the visitor came from (`#team/BUF`), else the top-rated team.
- **Reject.** Multi-team comparison mode. It is a second product for a use case (fantasy
  research) this dashboard is not for.
- **Defer.** Upcoming opponents beyond the current week: the page only has the current
  week's schedule.

### Season Accuracy

- **Current problem.** Strong scoreboard; weak trend chart (no x-axis week labels,
  overlapping end labels, drawn across part of the width); My Picks counted without a
  kickoff lock.
- **Desktop.** Keep the scoreboard exactly. Label the trend's weeks; stack end labels
  with a minimum gap. Count only picks locked before kickoff, and say how many were
  excluded if any.
- **Mobile.** As now.
- **Add.** A proper-scoring-rule row under the scoreboard once 50 or more games are
  graded (log loss for A, B and Market), because the page's own methodology says accuracy
  cannot carry the comparison.

### My Picks

- **Current problem.** Picks accepted after kickoff and after grading; most of the
  page's opening description is about where picks are stored.
- **Target.** A pick'em card you fill in before kickoff, with your record.
- **Design.** Lock each game at its kickoff: buttons disabled, a lock icon, and the pick
  stored with the time it was made. Picks imported from a share link keep their original
  times and are marked if they have none. The storage caveat becomes one line.

### Model Lab

- **Current problem.** A 46-row hand-written table, 11 labels, accuracy-led result
  sentences, no filter, and neither Stage 5 nor Stage 6. It opens on a dense reliability
  panel with four paragraphs of Brier decomposition before the first experiment.
- **Target.** "Everything we tried, how it came out, and how sure we are", scannable in
  ten seconds and auditable in ten minutes.
- **Desktop.**
  - Generated from structured records: one file of experiment entries (id, question,
    stage, decision, primary metric with interval, one-line result), with Stage 5 and 6
    read straight from `experiments/*/results/`. Hand-written rows move into the same
    file once, verbatim.
  - **Five decisions, as the project defines them** (ACCEPT, REJECT, INCONCLUSIVE,
    DEFERRED, CONFIRMED FINDING) plus one flag, *invalidated by leakage*, shown as an
    icon. The other six labels map onto these.
  - Filter chips by decision with counts; a search box.
  - The result column leads with the proper scoring rule and a small interval glyph
    (`├──●──┤` against a zero line), then the sentence.
  - The reliability diagram moves below the experiments, with its explanation behind
    "How to read this".
- **Mobile.** Each experiment becomes a compact card: name, decision chip, interval
  glyph, one line; tap to expand the full sentence.
- **Reject.** A timeline. Dates for most rows were never recorded, and What's Changed is
  already the chronological view.
- **Accessibility.** The interval glyph has a text equivalent ("−0.011, interval −0.032
  to +0.010, includes zero").

### Methodology

- **Current problem.** Long, but that is its job; one accuracy-led claim ("Model B
  consistently outperforms Model A (68.2% vs. 62.8% accuracy)") argues from the metric the
  project says cannot carry a result.
- **Change.** Restate the Model B vs Model A claim on log loss and Brier with intervals
  (the confirmed finding already exists in Model Lab). Keep the four-part structure and
  the page-jump row. Keep "Words used here" as the glossary; tooltips link into it.

### Checking the AI's work (verification)

- **Current problem.** Little. The four tiles are good; the incident prose is long.
- **Change.** Keep. Tighten the incident to three sentences with a link to its case
  study, as the other cards already do.

### What's Changed

- Keep. It is the model changelog the prompt asks for.

## 13. Features I recommend rejecting

Intelligence strip; error-analysis page; project tour; in-app architecture explorer;
command palette; collapsible sidebar and breadcrumbs; sticky time-slot filters; monospace
metadata font; skeleton loaders; print stylesheet; D3; pixel-diff visual regression;
multi-team comparison mode; conference/division filters; Model Lab timeline; saved
filters; export-to-image; model version comparison UI (What's Changed covers it).

The common reason: each adds a second way to do something the page already does, or
a surface to maintain whose value arrives only for a use case this product is not for.

## 15. Design system recommendations

- Keep the Stage 8 token layer (71 custom properties, four colour jobs, the type and
  spacing scales held by `tests/test_type_and_spacing_scale.py`).
- **Add a number-format layer**, as functions rather than tokens: `fmtProb` (one decimal,
  `%`), `fmtPP` (signed, one decimal, `pp`), `fmtRating` (signed, one decimal, per 100
  plays), `fmtInterval` (`[lo, hi]` in the value's own format), `fmtPoints` (pick'em
  only). A test bans `pt` and `pts` outside `fmtPoints`.
- **Add three components**: `probSpectrum`, `intervalGlyph`, `decisionChip` (five
  decisions plus the leakage flag). Each gets the treatment the existing components got:
  a guard, a mutation case, a render at 390 px.
- **Do not add layout primitives** (`.stack`, `.cluster`) as a separate layer now. The
  scales already give consistent spacing; primitives pay off in a codebase with many
  authors.

## 16. Frontend architecture recommendations

**Keep the single generated file and the single template, for now.** The template is
5,019 lines and 326 KB. That is large, but the cost of splitting it is higher than it
looks: the suite reads the template as one string in many places, the mutation corpus
anchors on exact strings in it, and the jargon guard parses its render functions. A
split would be one enormous mechanical PR whose only proof of correctness is "every test
was rewritten and still passes", which is the kind of claim this project distrusts.

What to do instead:

- **Group the template by section** with a table of contents comment at the top listing
  each CSS and JS section's purpose, so the file can be navigated.
- **Introduce render helpers inside the file** as components are touched
  (`probSpectrum`, `intervalGlyph`, `decisionChip`, the formatters), rather than in a
  refactoring pass.
- **Move data, not markup, out of the template.** Model Lab's hand-written rows become a
  data file the generator reads; that removes the template's largest block of
  hand-maintained content and its biggest source of drift.
- **Revisit the split at 7,000 lines**, and if it happens, make the build inline
  `styles/*.css` and `scripts/*.js` back into one file so deployment is unchanged, with
  a test that the built page is byte-identical before and after the split.

No ES modules, no bundler, no framework, no D3. SVG primitives as now.

## 17. Accessibility recommendations

1. `inert` (or `visibility:hidden` after the close transition) on the closed mobile
   sheet, so nothing off-screen is focusable. Must.
2. The `+ view context` toggle at least 24 px tall on touch (it is 13 px). Must.
3. The probability spectrum and interval glyph each carry a text equivalent. Must, with
   the components.
4. Model Lab's filter chips as a proper toggle group with `aria-pressed`. Should.
5. Keep the existing strengths: reduced motion, visible focus, non-colour encodings,
   `aria-sort`, the table system's keyboard sorting.

## 18. Performance recommendations

The site is already fast by construction: one HTML file, no runtime fetches, no
framework. Keep it. Add one budget test on the built file's size so growth is a decision,
not a drift (479 KB today). Logos are hot-linked from ESPN's CDN; that is a runtime
dependency on a third party that a static site does not need, and self-hosting 32 small
images would remove it. Nice to have.

## 19. Testing strategy

Highest value first:

1. **A browser job in CI** (Playwright on the Ubuntu runner, with the webfont files
   committed or fetched and asserted loaded by the measured widths rather than
   `document.fonts.check()`): no horizontal overflow at 360/390/768/1024/1280/1440; no
   focusable element outside the viewport or hidden; touch targets ≥ 24 px; axe-core with
   zero serious or critical violations on all nine pages. This is the check CLAUDE.md
   notes no CI job has today, and it would have caught two of the defects above.
2. **Integrity tests** for the new behaviour: a pick after kickoff is refused; a
   post-kickoff pick is excluded from the race.
3. **Model Lab's generated rows held to their result files**, the pattern
   `tests/test_stage6_published_numbers.py` set.
4. **Formatter tests**, including the ban on bare `pt`.

Not pixel-diff screenshots.

## 24. Exact recommended next implementation task

**Lock My Picks at kickoff.**

- A game's pick buttons are disabled from its kickoff (`gameday` + `gametime_et`,
  Eastern time, compared with the visitor's clock) with a lock icon and "Locked at
  kickoff" in the button's accessible name.
- A pick is stored with the time it was made. Existing stored picks have no time and
  are kept but marked unverified.
- Season Accuracy's My Picks row counts only picks made before their game's kickoff, and
  says how many it left out when any were.
- Picks imported from a share link keep their times; picks without one are shown as
  unverified and not counted.
- Tests: a pick after kickoff is refused; a legacy pick is not counted; the race count
  equals the number of locked picks; a mutation that removes the lock is caught.

Why first: it is the one place the dashboard can currently show a number that is not
true, on a product whose whole claim is that its numbers are. It is small, it touches
no model code, and it sets the standard for everything after it.
