# Planned 2026-11-11: league logos, MLB, March Madness and the NBA page

Mark's list in session 1111, planned with Claude and agreed the same afternoon. Numbers are frozen; the order of
work is not the order of the numbers.

**Order (Mark, 2026-11-11).** Stages 87 to 95 first, the day-sport v2 shadow included. Then Stage 104 (the NBA page)
right after Stage 87 item 4, ahead of Stage 95, because visitors see it today. Then Stage 102 (league logos), small

## Stage 97 - MLB data, decided before any model

1. A probe workflow reading schedule, results, probable pitchers and box scores from GitHub's runners: the MLB Stats
   API as the likely primary and Retrosheet game logs as the likely fallback, each tested on a runner rather than
   assumed (the NBA's own site refused cloud servers).
2. History depth per season, and both sources agreeing on one season, as `nfl_data_py` was checked.
3. A free market source for Model B (ESPN's public API, as the NHL used), or MLB ships Model A only and says so.
4. The starting-pitcher source and when it publishes.
5. Team identity across seasons (Cleveland's rename in 2022, the Athletics' move from Oakland to Sacramento).
6. Doubleheaders, suspended and postponed games.
7. A written go/no-go (an mlb-data document beside the NHL's and NBA's), and an amendment to decision 0006, which
   says "MLB not this year".

## Stage 98 - MLB's registration and pipeline

1. The registration, merged before any feature touches a real game: validation and held-out seasons, log loss
   first, the market as baseline if Stage 97 finds one.
2. Loader, schema check, cache and the MLB canary; schedule, live status and the day's slate.
3. Probable and confirmed starters stored per game, with where and when each was read.
4. Team colours and logos, measured for contrast and colour-blind separation like the other three sports.

## Stage 99 - MLB's model and its accuracy testing

1. Model A: team run-scoring and run-prevention strength, recency-weighted, plus a starting-pitcher term, refit on
   strictly earlier games, built on the shared day-sport core (day_ratings, day_backtest, day_drift, day_picks), not
   a fourth copy.
2. Model B if there is a market.
3. The backtest on validation then held-out seasons, the paired bootstrap at 5,000 for close calls, calibration,
   and the reproducibility audit with Booth re-running it on Linux; every published figure tied to its file.
4. Stated before the backtest: baseball is the noisiest of the four sports (favourites win only around 57 to 60%,
   even for the market), so accuracy will look weak beside the NFL's; with about 2,430 games a season the
   intervals are tight, and log loss and Brier decide.

## Stage 102 - League logos (Mark, 2026-11-11; reverses the 2026-10-09 decline)

On 2026-10-09 the leagues' official logos were declined for the home page (trademarks on a public site read as
endorsement; one icon set of our own instead), and the NFL's 32 team logos were never copied into the repository.
Mark decided on 2026-11-11 to use the league logos, knowing they are trademarks. Done the way the team logos are:

1. Each league's mark hot-linked from the league's or ESPN's own CDN, never committed to this public repository.
2. Our own icon as the fallback when the image fails to load.
3. One footer line: the site is not affiliated with or endorsed by the NFL, the NHL, the NBA or MLB.
4. The reversal and its reasoning in the Audit Response Log, so a re-audit does not raise it again.

## Stage 103 - March Madness bracket (Mark, 2026-11-11)

The men's tournament only, not the college season.

1. A season-long collector of men's college basketball results (ESPN, or hoopR's college data, with a checked
   fallback; no paid data), saving each game with when it was read, running by about January. The season's games
   are the ratings' input only: no season picks are published.
2. A registration before any result; a backtest on past tournaments (2015 to 2025, 2020 having none, about 670
   games) against "the higher seed wins" and, if a free archive exists, the market. A small sample: most
   comparisons are expected INCONCLUSIVE, and the page says so.
3. A simulation turning game probabilities into each team's odds of reaching each round, and one filled-in bracket.
4. One lock between Selection Sunday and the First Four, never rewritten, graded game by game; a bracket page and a
   home card that appear only in March. Dry-run before Selection Sunday, which falls about two weeks before MLB's
   Opening Day.

## Stage 104 - The NBA page, the same tabs (Mark, 2026-11-11)

The NBA page shipped (#321) as a backtest with no live picks, so it had Board, Model Lab, Methodology and Checking
the AI's work only. It has made and graded real picks every day since 2026-10-20, and a page with three weeks of
graded picks and no Season Accuracy reads as hiding the scoreboard. The uncertainty goes on the tabs, not in their
absence:

1. Season Accuracy: the forward test's record with its intervals, labelled a small and growing sample.
2. Power Ratings and Teams: what the model uses (the point, efficiency and availability terms).
3. What's Changed, with the "no board at launch, then live" history.
4. Standings odds under the NBA's rules (win percentage, its tiebreakers, the 7 to 10 play-in), with a test of the
   play-in seeding, not the NHL's simulation relabelled.
5. Model Lab keeps H1 and H2 ACCEPT and H3 REJECT, now beside the record.
