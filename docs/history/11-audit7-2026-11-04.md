# Audit 7 (2026-11-04): Stages 91 to 96

Mark ran a seventh audit on 2026-11-04 ("Sportalytics Audit 7 — Model, Resilience, Testing", a Claude Docs page). Unlike
audits 1 to 6 it executed the system at `main` b917c52f: both backtests re-run, the forward-test, drift and calibration
files re-derived to six decimals, the daily runs driven end to end with one injected fault each, a 30-day operational
record from the API, and ten hand-written mutations. It scores the three categories it re-ran at 70 (model), 60
(resilience) and 64 (testing), against 86, 84 and 88 at audit 5, and puts the whole project at about 83.

Its headline: the published numbers are real and reproducible, and no saved pick was ever modified or committed after its
start. What it found is what the numbers are *of* (Model B frozen on 2025 data, an NBA feature that means different things
in the backtest and live, an undeclared market instrument), green runs that publish wrong data, and a suite that is 60%
string checks with no test tying a published file back to the code.

Every finding is a hypothesis until the item's own session reproduces it (CLAUDE.md, "An audit finding is a hypothesis,
not a defect"). The audit read `main` before #599 (s83e) and #600 (s85e) landed and before #601 (s85a) was opened, so
several items were already closed or in flight when it arrived; the response says so.

## Response, written the same night (Claude, for Mark's decision)

**Agreed without reservation:** Stage 91 items 1 to 3 (a green run that pushes conflict markers, wipes the schedule, or
throws away a saved slate) are the most serious findings here and go first, ahead of new building work. Stages 92 and 93
are the right shape. Item 25 (declare the market instrument) and items 41 to 42 (a golden-number regression and a pandas
pin assertion) are cheap and close real holes.

**Already done or in flight when the audit arrived:** item 22 is #600 (merged 2026-11-05: this season's recorded prices
reach Model B's training rows); item 43 is s85r (one re-derivation test per sport); item 49's shared feature table is s86t
(the four NHL end-to-end tests from about 158 s to 48 s); Stage 85 item 1's five looks are #601; T5's stale split is what
s86s makes the wrap-up check.

**Pushback, item by item:**

1. **Items 24, 27, 28, 29, 30 and 35 change the registered model mid-season, after the confirmation seasons and the first
   month of live results have been seen.** That is the forking path this project's methodology exists to prevent: each
   would move the published backtest figures and change what the forward test is scoring. Item 22 is different, because
   it made the code do what the registration already said. The proposal: write these as one registered "day-sport v2",
   tuned on validation only, saved beside the registered model as a shadow forecast from the day it is registered, and
   judged on the forward test at season end. Item 24's +0.357 skew is a measurement worth reproducing first: the 11-04
   session found the backtest's own `availability_table` disagreeing with a hand-rolled box-score availability on 79 of 92
   games, so the mechanism is plausible but not yet ours.
2. **Item 13 (`if: failure() || cancelled()`)** would alert on every run cancelled because a newer one superseded it.
   Better: give the long steps their own `timeout-minutes`, so a hung feed fails the step (and alerts) instead of
   cancelling the job.
3. **Item 20 (the Pages build refuses a stale schedule)** would turn a missed slot into a missing site. It should alert
   from the build, not refuse to publish.
4. **Item 33's "suspiciously good" issue**: a live log loss better than the baseline by more than the season spread is
   worth a line on the page and in the run log; an issue for it would mostly be noise in a 194-game sample.
5. **Item 31's power guard** ("+0.02 flags at least 80% by n = 400") is a property to measure and report, not one a
   guard can promise; the null false-alarm bound (at most 7%) is the right guard. Indexing the baseline by games into the
   season is sound and lands as a second amendment after #601, disclosed as written after both had been read.
6. **Item 51 (a file-to-class map for the suite line)** would be a hand-kept map of 400 files that goes stale on every
   new test file. The two-way split the wrap-up now checks (s86s) carries the honest headline; the audit's five-way
   classification belongs in a one-off report, not a gate.
7. **Item 47 (Booth fixtures weekly)** spends Claude usage on a schedule. Mark's call.
8. **Item 43 / Stage 85 item 6's tolerance**: the audit saw two non-leaked picks differ by 0.0002 in its environment, so
   s85r's four-place equality may be flaky on CI's Linux runner. Adjusted: s85r compares within 5e-4.

**Order proposed:** 91 (items 1 to 3 first, one PR each), then 92 and 93 together, then 94's disclosure and
instrument items (23, 25, 26) and 95's drift items (31 to 34, 36 to 40), with the model-changing items held for Mark's
decision on the shadow-model proposal; 96 alongside. Before 11-19: items 23, 25 and 31, so the November figures say what
they are of.

### Stage 91 - A green run never publishes wrong data

1. The commit step refuses a dirty autostash: after `pull --rebase --autostash`, fail if a stash remains or a staged file holds a conflict marker, in all four writers (R1).
2. Refuse an implausibly small schedule: fewer games than the committed schedule minus a tolerance, or zero in season, raises before anything is written and prints `DEGRADED: schedule:` (R2).
3. Nothing after `save_once` can lose the slate: standings, grading, forward test and M2 each print `DEGRADED: <step>:` on failure and the run exits 0 with the picks on disk, or the workflow commits predictions first (R3).
4. The site refuses a sport whose schedule has zero games in season, and a kept live page carries a "stale since" line (R2, R14).
5. `save_once` refuses a record whose `training_through` is on or after its slate, whose market read is after its start, or whose `saved_utc` is after its start (M5).
6. NHL and NBA picks are written through `src/core/atomic_write.py` with fsync and a unique temporary name (R16).

### Stage 92 - Every silent downgrade says DEGRADED

7. An empty or unrecognised market payload prints `DEGRADED: odds feed:` when a game is locking; Kalshi's "not read" and the goalie page's "no payload" become DEGRADED lines (R4, R6).
8. NBA injuries: zero rows from a non-empty report raises and the pick records `read: false` (R5).
9. The canary executes every step it names, and its market step fails on zero priced games on a slate day (R4, T7).
10. One malformed price row drops that row, not the whole market (R12).
11. MISSED PICKS covers every game of the last two slate days that started without a pick (R11).

### Stage 93 - Alerts that cannot be lost

12. `alerts._gh` retries with backoff and, failing that, writes the body to the step summary and an error annotation and exits non-zero (R8).
13. A timed-out job alerts (R9); see the response above for the shape.
14. Every unattended workflow is in the alert-guard class, `workflow_run` and `pull_request` writers included; `deploy-pages.yml` and `nfl-override-watch.yml` get job-level alerts (R13).
15. Per-source canary titles are static and each has a resolver; the `$` exemption in `tests/test_alerts.py` goes (R7).
16. The daily failure body names the artifact that holds the picks and says to commit it before the first start, never after (R18).
17. `docs/operations.md`: one page per failure class with its recovery (R18, Stage 89 item 5).
18. The retrying fetch is passed to both sports' `schedule.fetch_json`, with a per-run request budget under the job timeout (R12).
19. Each GitHub copy's lateness is recorded, and the weekly digest fails when the 30-day maximum is within an hour of the lock slack (R10).
20. A missed-slot detector that does not depend on GitHub's cron (R10); see the response above for the shape.
21. A postcondition step in each daily workflow: `predictions/<sport>` shows only added files against `origin/main` (R16).

### Stage 94 - The live model is the registered model

24. NBA availability defined identically in the backtest and live (M2). Held: see the response above.
25. The market instrument is declared and recorded: lock price live, closing in history; the forward test reports which (M3).
26. A direction test for both day sports: a team that wins every game gets a positive matchup and a home probability above 0.5 (T1).

28. Neutral-site games drop the home edge in NHL and NBA, registered (M10). Held.
29. Market and box-score sanity at ingest: impossible overrounds and short boxes are marked, not used (M11). Held.
30. The 2020-03-01 double-GS slate is fixed and no team appears twice on one slate (M11). Held.

### Stage 95 - Drift that means something
